#### [🔗 Square Peg in a Round Hole: AirPower against Mobile Targets and Missiles](https://secretaryrofdefenserock.substack.com/p/square-peg-in-a-round-hole-airpower)

> [...] the targets’ airpower struggles with a common signature problem. Static, defensively oriented forces emit little heat, noise, or radio traffic, and from an aircraft at 10,000 feet a half-buried tank is hard to distinguish from “a sheet of corrugated metal half-buried in the sand.” The problem compounds in two ways. First, the same low signature that makes a stationary vehicle hard to find makes a freshly destroyed vehicle hard to distinguish from a live one, leading to repeated strikes on the same target and inflated battle-damage estimates. Second, cheap decoys exploit exactly this ambiguity; Iraqi forces, Press notes, learned to place wreckage next to operational vehicles to make them appear already destroyed.

#### [🔗📺&#x1F1EC;&#x1F1E7; *Gone* (2026)](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt39019156/?ref_=ext_shr_lnk)

A UK crime drama that doesn't quite live up to expectations.

David Morrisey stars as the stereotypical headmaster of a local public school[1] whose wife (also a teacher there) goes missing. As usual revelations and twists are exposed over 6 well-executed hours.

The actors and settings are good, but the details aren't. Why is the driven DI played by Eve Myles sidelined as an FLO[2] to the bereaved headmaster, and why is she still there when the headmaster (SPOILER!) becomes a suspect? Why are multiple interviews conducted without a solicitor present? 

Worth a view but a bit disappointing. 

Viewed on SVT streaming.

---

[1] This is England, so this means private school in the rest of the world. 

[2] Family Liaison Officer, very popular character in UK crime shows.
